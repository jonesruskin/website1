Script attributes and theming

The data attributes the embed script reads, and how to make the card match your site.

The embed is a single script tag. Configuration lives in data- attributes on it.

Attributes

AttributeValuesDefaultWhat it does
data-keyyour bk_… site keynoneRequired. Identifies your tool.
data-ctxan artifact id, e.g. pdfnoneDefault context for every baton.pass() call on the page.
data-themeauto, light or darkautoThe card's color scheme. auto follows the visitor's system setting.
data-positioninline or toastinlineinline draws the card in the page; toast shows it as a small pop-up card.

A ctx passed directly to baton.pass() overrides data-ctx.

The card is a web component

The card renders as a custom element, <baton-card>, and its markup and styles live in a Shadow DOM. That has two consequences:

  • Your CSS can't restyle the inside of the card by accident, and the card's CSS can't leak into your page.
  • The card stays readable on any background, because it brings its own surface, text and focus styles.

Light, dark and auto

Use data-theme to choose the card's scheme:

  • auto follows the visitor's system preference (prefers-color-scheme).
  • light and dark force one scheme, for example when your site has its own theme switch.

Custom properties

Custom properties inherit through the Shadow DOM boundary, so you can set them on the baton-card element, or on any ancestor, from your normal stylesheet. Three are supported:

PropertyWhat it controls
--baton-accentThe accent color: the button and highlights.
--baton-radiusThe card's corner radius.
--baton-fontThe font family used inside the card.

Those three are the whole styling surface. There is no way to reach into the card's internals, and that is deliberate: it keeps every card recognisable, legible and accessible, wherever it appears.

Plans

Custom properties work on every plan. Hiding the "passed by Baton" mark is part of the Anchor plan and above.

Where to load the script

Put the script in the <head> (or, in a framework, wherever you load third-party scripts) and keep async. It is small, doesn't block rendering, and does nothing until you call pass(). See the framework recipes for React, Next.js, Vue, Svelte and plain HTML.
